# Why Your Gut Feeling is Probably Wrong (According to the Data)

*A look at the patterns, the "Save Paradox," and why FPL success is more about memory than intuition.*

---

Let's be honest: Most Fantasy Premier League managers are result-chasers. We see a player score a banger on Match of the Day, and by Sunday night, they’re in our team. But behind the scenes of the **FPL Geek** engine, the data is telling a different, much more cynical story. 

If you want to win, you have to stop thinking like a fan and start thinking like a machine. Not a cold, emotionless robot, but one with an "Intelligent Memory."

### The 10-Game Marathon vs. The 4-Game Sprint
The biggest trap in FPL is the "purple patch." A player scores two weeks in a row, and we think they're essential. Our AI doesn't fall for that. It splits its attention between the immediate momentum of the last four games and the cold reality of the last ten.

Why ten games? Because it’s the ultimate "noise filter." It identifies players who are consistently finding space in the box, even if the ball hasn't hit the net yet. The machine rewards reliability over luck. If a player is a roller-coaster—bagging 15 points then disappearing for a month—the model flags them as a liability. It’s looking for the steady climbers, the players who give you a solid foundation so you can afford to take risks elsewhere.

### The "Save Paradox": Why Bad Teams Can Be Good for Keepers
If you’re picking a goalkeeper based on how "world-class" their defense is, you might be leaving points on the table.

Our analysis revealed what we call the **Save Paradox**. A keeper for a team like Arsenal might be incredible, but if they face zero shots, they get zero save points. Meanwhile, a keeper for a scrappy mid-table side facing ten shots and saving nine is a total goldmine. The AI prioritizes "Shot Volume"—it wants keepers who are busy, playing behind a defense that is solid enough to avoid a 5-0 thrashing but vulnerable enough to keep their keeper on their toes.

### The Midfield Trap: Assists are a Myth
We all love a creative playmaker, but if you’re looking for a double-digit "haul," you need to ignore the assists and follow the **Goal Threat.**

Creativity is fragile. A player can put in five perfect crosses, but if his striker is having a bad day, he gets zero points. Goal Threat is different. It’s about the player’s own intent—their ability to take shots from high-quality positions. In the midfield, the machine prioritizes "out of position" attackers every single time. If a midfielder isn’t getting into the box, they’re not on the radar. 

And here’s a tip the AI found: In the midfield, you usually get what you pay for. The correlation between a high price tag and massive returns is strongest here. If you're going to spend big, spend it on your mids.

### The 90-Minute Rule
For forwards, the AI is remarkably simple: **If they don't play, they don't score.** 

It sounds obvious, but how many times have we gambled on a "super-sub" or a player who gets hooked at the 60th minute? The machine treats playing time as a binary switch. If a striker isn't a "90-minute man," their chance of a haul is slashed immediately. In the forward line, minutes aren't just a stat—they are the absolute floor for any potential big score.

### How to Manage More Like a Machine
You don't need a PhD in data science to use these insights. Just change your perspective:
*   **Back the "Unlucky" Ones:** Look for players who have huge Goal Threat but have been unlucky with their actual returns over the last 10 games. They are "due," and the algorithm knows it.
*   **Save Money on Defense:** Budget defenders are where you find your value; budget midfielders are where you find your regret.
*   **The 60% Test:** If you aren't 100% sure a player will finish the full 90, find someone else. 

FPL is a game of probability. The AI doesn't have a "gut feeling," and neither should you. Trust the long-term patterns, watch the save volumes, and stop chasing last week's points.
